Threat Catalog

Common Cyber Threats

The digital landscape holds traps. Here is how modern cyber threats operate and the precise counter-moves to dismantle them.

Phishing Attacks

High Severity

Deceptive emails or fake messages pretending to be trusted brands to steal passwords or banking credentials.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Check sender email addresses carefully, never click unexpected verification links, and verify directly on official apps.

Malware & Ransomware

Critical

Malicious software that sneaks onto your device via infected downloads, encrypting files or spying on keystrokes.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Install verified antivirus, keep OS updated, and only download programs from legitimate, official stores.

Social Engineering

Psychological

Psychological manipulation where scammers pose as friends, tech support, or professors in distress to extract funds or sensitive data.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Pause and verify identity through a secondary channel before sending money, codes, or private credentials.

Credential Stuffing

Automated

Automated bots testing stolen password lists across multiple platforms, breaking into accounts reusing simple phrases.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Use unique 14+ character passphrases per service and rely on an encrypted password manager.

Public Wi-Fi Snooping

Network MITM

Unsecured campus or cafe hotspots allowing rogue actors to intercept network traffic and unencrypted logins.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Avoid accessing bank or confidential accounts on public hotspots, or always route through a trusted VPN.

Identity Theft & Leaks

Criminals collecting fragmented personal details (birthdays, pet names, student IDs) across social platforms to impersonate you.

HOW TO STAY SAFE:

Audit privacy settings, limit publicly visible profile information, and freeze credit profiles if compromised.

Hygiene Checklist

Stay Safe Online

Small daily micro-habits compound into an impenetrable shield. Adopt these 7 daily rules.

1

Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication

Enable MFA/2FA on every primary email, social, and student account.

2

Instant Skepticism for Urgency

Treat urgent or panic-inducing messages with immediate caution.

3

Patch OS Automatically

Keep phone and laptop operating systems updated to close zero-day exploits.

4

Avoid Unknown Public USBs

Never plug in unknown USB sticks or charging cords found in libraries or cafes.

5

Audit App Permissions

Revoke unnecessary camera, microphone, and location permissions regularly.

6

Encrypted Cloud & Drive Backups

Back up student assignments and documents so ransomware cannot hold them hostage.

7

Wipe Public Lab Sessions

Always explicitly log out of shared computers and clear cached credentials.

The Rule of Unique Passwords

Using one password everywhere creates a single point of catastrophic failure. When a minor fitness app leaks their database, automated bots immediately test those same credentials across your Gmail, bank, and university portals.

The Power of MFA

99.9% Shield

Multi-Factor Authentication pairs something you know (your passphrase) with something you have (authenticator app or hardware key). Even if an attacker steals your password, they are stopped cold.

Scam Detection

Phishing Awareness

Attackers do not break into systems; they simply log in with tricked credentials. Dissecting an authentic phishing trap:

From: security@paypa1-verify.com10:42 AM
URGENT: Your account will be closed in 24 hours!
Dear Valued Customer, please download invoice.zip to preserve your account privileges...
SUSPICIOUS VERIFY BUTTON

Deceptive Lookalike Domain

Notice paypa1-verify.com substituted a '1' for an 'l'. Legit organizations use their exact primary domain.

Manufactured Urgency

"24 hours until closure" forces panic to bypass logical verification.

Generic Greeting

"Dear Valued Customer" reveals high-volume automated blast instead of real account handling.

Hazardous Attachments

Unsolicited .zip, .html, or .exe payloads disguise malware installers.

THE 5-SECOND GOLDEN RULE

When in doubt, never click the link. Open a completely fresh browser tab and manually navigate to the official website directly.

Clarity & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common beginner cybersecurity inquiries.

Do I need to be a coding genius?

No. Most of the everyday protection in this guide needs no coding at all. Coding becomes useful later, when you want to build hands-on projects.

Is built-in antivirus enough?

For many people, built-in protection is a solid baseline. Keep it updated, and add the other habits here, such as MFA and careful link checking.

Are password managers really safe?

Reputable password managers encrypt your vault, and they are generally far safer than reusing passwords. Protect the vault itself with a strong master passphrase and MFA.

What if I clicked a phishing link?

Stay calm. Change the affected password right away from the official site, turn on MFA, check for unfamiliar logins, and tell your bank or institution if financial details were entered.

How to land my first cyber role?

Build a portfolio with documented projects from the roadmap above, learn the fundamentals, and practice on hands-on labs and CTF challenges before applying.
